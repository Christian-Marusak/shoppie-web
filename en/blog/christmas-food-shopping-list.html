Christmas food shopping list: how to plan it and waste less

Published 23 November 2026 · 4 min read

In short

The easiest way to make a Christmas food shopping list is to start from a meal plan: write down what you will cook over the holidays and for how many people, check what you already have at home, and list only what is missing. Buy long-life items 3–4 weeks ahead and fresh food just 1–2 days before Christmas Eve.

Christmas is when we buy the most food – and throw the most away. Potato salad, bread and baked treats often end up in the bin simply because we bought or made them “just in case”. A good Christmas shopping list saves you money, time in crowded shops and food you would otherwise waste. Here is how to put one together in six steps.

Step 1: Write a holiday meal plan

Don’t start with a list of groceries – start with a list of meals. Go through the holidays day by day:

  • what you will serve for the main Christmas meal,
  • what you will cook on the other holiday days,
  • when guests are coming and what you will offer them,
  • which days you won’t be at home, for example at family – no cooking needed then.

Once you know what you will eat, you know what to buy. No more shopping blind.

Step 2: Count portions, not “just in case”

The most common reason food gets thrown away at Christmas is that we make too much of it. For each meal, write down how many people you are cooking for and plan the amount accordingly. It also helps to think back to last Christmas: what was left over, and what did you end up throwing away? Buy less of that this year.

The same goes for baking: a few kinds in smaller batches beat ten full trays, half of which go stale by January.

Step 3: Check what you already have

Before you go shopping, go through your fridge, freezer and cupboards. Flour, sugar, nuts and spices are often left over from last time. Cross off everything you already have. A handy trick: take a photo of your fridge before you go, so you don’t buy things twice.

Step 4: Split your shopping by shelf life

You don’t have to buy everything in one go the week before Christmas. Splitting it up helps you avoid the biggest crowds, and your fresh food will really be fresh.

WhenWhat to buy
3–4 weeks aheadflour, sugar, nuts, dried fruit, baking chocolate, spices, tins, drinks
1–2 weeks aheadbutter (it freezes well), frozen food, cured meats and cheeses with a longer shelf life
2–3 days aheadvegetables, fruit, dairy, eggs
The day beforebread, pastries, fresh fish or meat, herbs

Over the holidays, shops have shorter opening hours or close completely. Check your shop’s opening times in advance and don’t leave the big shop until Christmas Eve.

Step 5: Shop together as a family

At Christmas, several people often shop at the same time, and it is easy for two of you to buy the same thing. Keep one shared list that everyone in the household can add to. Whoever is at the shop buys what is missing and ticks it off, so everyone else can see straight away what is already at home.

Step 6: Plan for leftovers

Even with the best plan, something will be left over. Plan for it in advance:

  • plan a couple of leftover meals for the days after Christmas, such as a bake or a soup,
  • soups, cooked meat and most baked goods can be frozen – do it straight away, not when they start to go off,
  • keep an eye on the best-before dates of food in the fridge and eat what goes off first.

Frequently asked questions

When should I buy food for Christmas?

Buy long-life items such as flour, sugar, nuts and drinks 3–4 weeks ahead. Butter, frozen food and cured meats 1–2 weeks ahead, vegetables, fruit and dairy 2–3 days ahead, and bread, pastries and fresh fish or meat the day before Christmas Eve.

What should be on a Christmas food shopping list?

Ingredients for your main Christmas meal, baking ingredients, food for the other holiday days, something for guests, and drinks. Always build the exact list from your own meal plan and from what you already have at home.

How can I reduce food waste at Christmas?

Cook for the number of people you expect rather than “just in case”, check your cupboards before shopping, buy fresh food as late as possible, plan meals for leftovers and freeze what you won’t eat in time.

Can Christmas food be frozen?

Yes, much of it. Soups, cooked meat and most baked goods freeze well. Freeze them as soon as possible, while they are still fresh.
