How to make room in the fridge for Christmas food

Published 27 November 2026 · 4 min read

In short

To make room in the fridge for Christmas, start 2 to 3 weeks ahead: eat up what is running low, throw out anything spoiled or past its use-by date, clean the shelves and move drinks and hardy produce elsewhere. You will go into the big shop knowing what you have and with free shelves.

In the run-up to the holidays the fridge fills up fast: the roast, trays of sides, cheeses, desserts and everything that was already in there. If you are wondering how to make room in the fridge for Christmas, the trick is not to leave it until Christmas Eve. Here is a simple plan that frees up space for the festive food without throwing away anything that could still be eaten.

When to start: 2 to 3 weeks before

Early December is ideal. It leaves enough time to eat through what you have rather than binning it. About two weeks before Christmas, stop buying extra "just in case" and cook from what is already at home.

  • Early December: take stock and plan a "leftovers week".
  • The week before Christmas: clean the fridge and freezer.
  • The day before the big shop: a final check and clear the shelves.

Step 1: Take everything out and sort it

Empty the fridge shelf by shelf and sort the food into three piles:

  • Eat soon – opened cheese, yoghurts, vegetables, leftovers.
  • Keep – butter, eggs, mustard and anything you will use over the holidays.
  • Throw out – anything spoiled, mouldy or past its use-by date.

Know the difference between dates: food past its "best before" date is often still fine if it looks and smells right and has been stored properly. With meat, fish and dairy past the "use by" date, do not take the risk.

Step 2: Clean the shelves and check the temperature

Switch the empty fridge off, wash the shelves and drawers in warm soapy water and dry them well. A small bowl of bicarbonate of soda helps with smells. UK food safety guidance says your fridge should be 5°C or below, and a cheap fridge thermometer is the easiest way to check, because the dial does not always show the real temperature.

Step 3: Free up space for the festive food

A lot of space goes on things that do not need to be in the fridge:

  • Drinks – move wine, soft drinks and water to a pantry, garage or a cool box.
  • Fruit and veg – apples, citrus, onions, potatoes and tomatoes do not need the fridge.
  • Unopened jars and sauces – until they are opened, the cupboard is fine.
  • The freezer – freeze bread, stock or meat for New Year. For more on freezing, see How to store Christmas cookies.

Step 4: Pack the fridge with a plan

As the fridge fills up, keep these rules in mind:

  • Put raw meat and fish on the bottom shelf so nothing drips onto ready-to-eat food.
  • Let hot food cool before it goes in, so it does not warm up everything around it.
  • Leave a little space between items so cold air can circulate.
  • Square containers stack better than round bowls.
  • Keep leftovers at the front so you do not forget them.

Step 5: Buy only what is missing

After the clear-out you know exactly what you have. Write the missing items on your Christmas food shopping list and skip anything already in the cupboard. You save money as well as space.

Frequently asked questions

When should I clear out the fridge before Christmas?

Ideally 2 to 3 weeks ahead. Start by eating up what you have, and leave the deep clean for the week before Christmas or the day before your big shop.

Where can I keep food when the fridge is full at Christmas?

Move drinks, unopened jars and hardy vegetables to a pantry, garage or cool box. Keep meat, fish and cooked dishes in the fridge, as outdoor temperatures change during the day.

What temperature should my fridge be?

UK food safety guidance recommends 5°C or below. Check it with a fridge thermometer.

Can I put hot food straight in the fridge?

Let it cool first. A hot pan warms up the food around it.
