How to save money on Christmas food: practical tips

Published 25 November 2026 · 4 min read

In short

How to save money on Christmas food? Decide how much you want to spend, plan the menu for the number of guests, check what you already have and shop from a list. Buy long-life items early when they are on offer, fresh food just before the holidays, and cook fewer dishes.

Christmas is the most expensive shopping season of the year, and food is a big part of the bill. If you know how to save money on Christmas food, you don't have to give up the roast, the trimmings or your favourite cookies. All it takes is a plan, a little discipline in the shop and buying things at the right time. Here is a simple method you can start this week.

Start with a figure and a menu, not the shop

Before you open the first leaflet, decide how much you want to spend on holiday food. Then write down a menu for every festive day: Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, Boxing Day and the breakfasts in between. Note how many people each meal is for.

  • Cook fewer dishes. Two kinds of meat, three salads and ten types of cookies are expensive and usually end up as leftovers.
  • Skip what nobody eats. If no one really likes a traditional dish, there is no need to buy it out of habit.
  • Plan leftovers in advance. Roast meat can become sandwiches the next day, and a big pot of soup can cover two lunches.

Check your cupboards first

It is easy to buy flour, sugar, spices or tins you already have. Go through the pantry, fridge and freezer and write down what needs using up and what is actually missing. Forgotten freezer stock such as meat, fruit or vegetables can go straight into the Christmas menu.

Turn recipes into a shopping list

Copy the ingredients from your menu into a list, with quantities. Split it into two parts: what you can buy early and what to buy just before the holidays. With a list in hand you are less likely to fall for festive displays and impulse buys at the till. For ideas, see our Christmas food shopping list.

Spreading the shopping over several weeks also spreads the cost, so one December payday doesn't have to cover everything. You also avoid the packed shops in the last days before Christmas, when impulse buying is at its peak.

Use offers wisely

A deal only saves money if you were going to buy that item anyway.

  • Compare the price per kilo or litre, not the pack price. A bigger pack isn't always cheaper.
  • Check the leaflets of the shops you normally use, and use loyalty cards or store apps that give extra discounts.
  • Try own-brand products for basics like flour, sugar, butter for baking or tins.
  • Be careful with multibuy deals on fresh food. The extra pack often goes off before you eat it.

For groceries, there is no need to chase one-off sales like Black Friday. What matters are the regular weekly offers, which multiply before Christmas.

Fewer leftovers means more money saved

The most expensive food is the food you throw away. Cook portions for the number of people, refill serving dishes gradually and get leftovers into the fridge or freezer quickly. Cookies you can't finish last longer when stored properly. Read how to store Christmas cookies.

Frequently asked questions

How can I save money on my Christmas food shop?

Set a spending limit, plan the menu for the number of guests, check what you already have and shop from a list. Buy long-life ingredients early on offer and cook fewer dishes.

When should I buy food for Christmas?

Buy baking ingredients, tins and drinks in late November or early December. Meat can be bought on offer and frozen. Leave fresh food until a few days before the holidays.

Is it worth stocking up on food deals before Christmas?

Yes, for long-life food you will really use, such as flour, sugar or tins. For fresh food, only buy what you can eat in time.

How do I make a cheap Christmas dinner?

Build the meal around affordable ingredients like potatoes, root vegetables and a cheaper cut of meat. Cook fewer dishes and match portions to the number of people at the table.
